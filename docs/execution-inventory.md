# Execution inventory recovery

Execution receipts are split between `executions/active` and `executions/history`. The active directory is bounded and consulted for scheduler admission; history remains addressable by execution ID and is not scanned during admission.

Inventory updates use `executions/.inventory.lock`. If the lock owner is stale or cannot be verified, autodev fails closed and leaves the lock in place. It does not remove a lock by path because another process could acquire a replacement between a stale check and unlink.

Ordinary observer writes, terminal persistence, migration, and inventory reads share this lock. When an inventory read detects contention, including the brief empty-lock initialization window, inventory remains protected and diagnostic; scheduler admission returns `deferred` without blocking the pending task or creating a claim. An observer skips a contended ordinary flush and retries its latest in-memory state on the next event or sample. It does not infer backend failure from contention. A retained lock whose recorded owner is still alive can also defer indefinitely and requires the recovery procedure below. Stale or oversized/nonregular owner evidence remains an inventory error. Temporary receipt files left after a crash remain protected errors once the lock is available; they are never ignored.

If contention begins after the fresh read but before observer initialization, the scheduler also defers without starting a worker, recording a RunDb attempt/cost, invalidating preflight, creating a learning outcome, or consuming the one-time alternative retry. It releases only this cycle's new claim and keeps the clean prepared worktree for the existing next-cycle validation/recreation flow. Other initialization failures remain explicit pre-dispatch blocks without worker billing. TeamState's existing admission counter is preserved: a claimed but deferred admission can consume a `dailyAttemptCap` slot, even though no worker ran. With a cap of one, this can defer work until the next UTC day. Actual engine failures retain their normal accounting.

Verified terminal receipts are retained indefinitely in history; this change does not delete receipts or introduce a cleanup schedule. Each inventory read bounds traversal of each legacy or active directory to 40,033 entries and receipt parsing in each directory to 20,000 files of at most 16 KiB. History is excluded from that traversal. Direct execution-ID lookup checks only active, history, and legacy paths. At 10,000 retained active receipts, admission reports `execution-inventory-capacity`; uncertain receipt or backend evidence remains `team-state-quarantined`. Quarantined data keeps admission protected even when a migration marker is missing after a failed write or partial restore.

If terminal persistence fails, or both archival and persistence of the recovery state fail, the inventory lock remains as a durable protection fence. A completed source change may already have been merged at this point, but the scheduler retains and quarantines its team claim until receipt recovery is verified. A storage failure does not prove the backend is still running.

To recover a stale or retained lock, stop the scheduler first. Read the lock owner fields (`pid`, `processStartedAt`, and `acquiredAt`) and verify that the recorded process no longer owns the inventory. If the owner is malformed or its state cannot be verified, keep the scheduler stopped until an operator can establish that no writer is active. For a failed terminal update, also verify the retained receipt and backend result; keep any quarantined team claim until that recovery is confirmed. After confirming there is no live owner, remove only `executions/.inventory.lock` and restart the scheduler. Do not delete or edit files under `active`, `history`, or `quarantine` as part of lock recovery.
